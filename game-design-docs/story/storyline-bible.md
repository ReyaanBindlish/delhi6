# DHURANDHAR: THE GAME — Storyline Bible

Nov 8, 2026 · Tarun Bindlish

## Logline and tone

**Logline:** A Delhi gangster who lost everything and ended up begging at Nizamuddin station is recruited by Indian intelligence precisely because he is invisible, sent into Karachi's underworld under a dead man's name, and comes home a hero nobody can acknowledge to find his entire family murdered while he was away.

**Working title:** DHURANDHAR: THE GAME. Part 1 is subtitled *Katora* (the begging bowl), Part 2 *Wapsi* (the return). Note: "Dhurandhar" is the title of a released film franchise, so shipping under this name needs a licence from the rights holders; the subtitles work as standalone fallbacks.

**One-line theme:** Money made him visible, poverty made him invisible, the state made him useful, and none of the three ever made him safe.

**Tone reference (vibe, not plot):** the slow-burn, grimy undercover register of the Dhurandhar films. Long stretches of silence and routine; violence that is sudden, ugly and over in seconds; Karachi as a real lived-in city (tea stalls, power cuts, Rangers checkpoints, mohalla politics) rather than a shooting gallery; the handler relationship as the emotional spine. No one-liners, no slow-motion. The player should feel that every character, including the hero, could die in any scene.

**Player experience in three words:** survive, pass, lose.

**Structure:** two parts, six acts, told strictly in chronological order with no flashbacks and no cold open; roughly 14 to 18 hours in total. The only backward movement in the whole game is the rewind montage as Daya dies. The game is linear in story but open in approach inside each act (how you make money, how you build cover, how you get out).

**Rating target:** 18+. Violence, drugs, abject poverty, no sexual content.

## Two-part structure

The game is shaped like a two-film spy saga: Part 1 is the infiltration and ends on a cliffhanger, Part 2 is the return and the revenge. The difference is that the hero's origin, which such films usually hold back and reveal as a prologue in the second part, here opens Part 1 and is never jumped back to. The player lives it, then lives the consequences, in order.

| Part | Acts | Where it starts | Where it ends | Running time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Part 1 — Katora | 1 to 4 | The collection run, Ghaziabad, 2019 | Qureshi crossing out three of four names after the Rangers raid. Daya has 72 hours. Hard cut to black. | 9 to 11 hours |
| Part 2 — Wapsi (the return) | 5 to 6 | The run to the Ibrahim Hyderi jetty, the same night | The pavement below the Vasundhara balcony, 2025 | 5 to 7 hours |

**If shipped as two releases:** Part 1 stands alone as a rise-fall-infiltration story with a cliffhanger. Part 2 opens exactly where Part 1 cut, with a 60-second "previously" that runs forward, never back. Part 2's title, *Wapsi*, carries both meanings: the return home and the return of the past.

**If shipped as one release:** the Part 1 end card still plays, with a two-minute interval. The disease, the pension trust, Bablu, the thumb tell and the katta under the Loni tree are all planted in Part 1 and all paid off in Part 2; nothing in Part 2 is introduced new except the diagnosis.

## The story in order

Every beat of the game, in the order the player plays it. Nothing is shown out of sequence.

1. **Family, 2019.** Daya at home in Vasundhara with Sukhi, Gurnoor and Bibi. Money, a Fortuner, a nine-year-old who wants a real cricket bat. (Act 1)
2. **The gangster, 2019.** The collection run, the wedding shooting, the hawala call. Daya and Bablu run Ghaziabad. (Act 1)
3. **The arrest.** A new SSP, Bablu's betrayal, ₹11 lakh in the car on the Loni flyover. Dasna jail. (Act 1)
4. **The fall, 2020 to 2021.** Property attached, beaten in jail, released to a one-room rental in Khoda Colony. Every job door closes. The e-rickshaw loan. (Act 2)
5. **The beggar, 2021 to 2022.** Nizamuddin station. Mangal teaches him the platform. Hunger, police drives, ₹300 on a good day. He learns he is invisible. (Act 2)
6. **The spymaster, 2022.** A quiet old man in a kurta buys chai opposite his spot for three days, then asks in Lahori Punjabi where his grandmother was born. Devraj Khanna, the national security chief, has found his asset. (Act 2 end)
7. **The pitch.** Ravi Saxena, the handler, lays out the Sattar network and the terms: ₹60,000 a month to the family and the school fees. Daya says yes. (Act 3)
8. **The farm.** Four months near Dehradun becoming Dilawar Hussain of Kasur. Khanna's one night visit. (Act 3)
9. **The goodbye.** A lie about trucks in Oman. The real bat for Gurnoor. Bibi's Urdu letter: if you see the house, do not go in. (Act 3)
10. **The route in.** Kathmandu, Sharjah, Karachi. (Act 3)
11. **Nobody, 2023.** Begging outside a shrine in Gulbahar Lines, carrying water, following the tankers to Sattar. The cough begins. (Act 4)
12. **Runner.** Shahzaib takes him on. Hawala from the other side. Qureshi of ISI drinks tea with Sattar and starts counting four names. (Act 4)
13. **Inside, 2024.** The beating he takes for Shahzaib. The ledger. Eleven minutes with Ravi at a cricket ground. Qureshi's eight-second question about Kasur station. The thumb tell. (Act 4)
14. **Sattar or Shahzaib.** The player's one big choice. The ledger is photographed either way. (Act 4)
15. **The raid.** The Rangers cordon Gulbahar Lines. Daya gets out without a weapon. Shahzaib is taken. (Act 4)
16. **The cliffhanger.** Qureshi crosses out three of four names. 72 hours. End of Part 1.
17. **The run.** Korangi checkpoint, the jetty, the tide. Ravi is shot on the shore and does not make the boat. Coast Guard pickup; Daya coughing blood into the sea. (Act 5)
18. **The return.** Nine days of debrief. The module is rolled up. Khanna's silence. Free to go home. (Act 5)
19. **The empty house.** Police tape, 16 days old. Sukhi, Gurnoor and Bibi, killed by two men from "the pension trust". (Act 6)
20. **What Khanna knew.** The agency knew in three days and chose the ledger over the warning. Confront him or never see him again. (Act 6)
21. **The hunt, eight months.** The tattoo, the muster roll, the Loni dhaba, the dying man's name: Bablu, on a Karachi number that leads back to Qureshi. (Act 6)
22. **The diagnosis.** Collapse at a bus stop. Advanced tuberculosis, a lung half full of fluid, perhaps a year. He stops the medicine because it slows him. (Act 6)
23. **The balcony, 2025.** Bablu's fourth-floor flat, the katta from under the Loni tree, the fight, the coughing fit, the 90 cm rail. Daya goes over. (Act 6)
24. **The rewind.** The fall from his eyes: the bat, the letter, the chalk mark, Ravi, the bowl, the Fortuner, the sky. The pavement, a dog, a shutter. (Act 6)
25. **Coda.** Qureshi writes Daya's real name. Khanna sends the pension to the Mamdot gurdwara forever. Mangal finds a ₹500 note. End of Part 2.

## Protagonist: Daya Dhillon

Daya is 38 when the game starts, 41 when it ends. He is the only playable character.

| | |
|---|---|
| Full name | Dayanand "Daya" Dhillon |
| Born | 1988, Mamdot, Ferozepur district, Punjab, 6 km from the border |
| Family history | Grandparents came from Kasur (now Pakistan) in 1947; Lahori Punjabi spoken at home. This is the entire reason he can pass. |
| Education | Dropped out in Class 10. Reads Gurmukhi and Urdu script, both taught by his grandmother. |
| Build | 5 ft 10, wiry. Loses 14 kg across Act 2; the model and animation set visibly change. |
| Tell | Cracks his left thumb when lying. The ISI antagonist notices it in Act 4. |
| Voice | Says little. Thinks in money: he counts everything, from bullets to chapatis. |

**Arc in one line:** a man who believed money was protection learns that nothing is, then has to decide whether that knowledge makes him free or empty.

**Skills that carry over from gangster to spy** (the game's justification for recruiting him):
- Fluent Lahori Punjabi and functional Urdu with a Kasur accent.
- Knows hawala: how money moves without moving. The Karachi mission is a money-trail mission, not a bomb-defusal mission.
- Knows how gang hierarchies recruit, test and kill. He has been on both ends.
- Comfortable with a pistol and a country-made katta; has never used a rifle. The game never magically teaches him one.
- No digital footprint. His Aadhaar is suspended, his phone is gone, his bank accounts are attached. On paper he barely exists.

**What he cannot do** (and the game never lets him): he cannot fight three men at once, cannot take a bullet and keep running, cannot hack anything, cannot drive a motorbike faster than a Karachi traffic cop. He is a patient, frightened, observant man who is good at being underestimated.

## Act 1 — The Gangster (Ghaziabad and East Delhi, 2019)

Act 1 is a 90-minute playable prologue that gives the player everything so Act 2 can take it away.

**Setup.** Daya and Bablu Tyagi run a mid-sized operation out of Loni and Sahibabad: illegal liquor across the UP border, builder-floor land grabs, protection for a transport union. Daya is the money man; Bablu is the muscle. Daya has a three-storey house in Vasundhara, a Fortuner, two phones, ₹4 crore in cash spread across six people and a hawala line to Dubai that he built himself.

**Playable beats.**
1. **The collection run.** Tutorial. Drive, talk, intimidate, count money. Teaches the player the money system: every rupee in the game is tracked and the HUD shows cash on hand, not a score.
2. **The wedding.** A rival gang's shooter walks into Daya's cousin's wedding. The player handles it with a single shot or a bribe. Introduces the realism rule: a pistol at a wedding ends the wedding, draws police within four minutes, and costs ₹3 lakh in settlements.
3. **The hawala call.** Daya moves ₹80 lakh to Dubai by phone in under a minute. The player watches the chain of tokens and code words that will matter in Act 4.
4. **Home.** The player walks the Vasundhara house with Gurnoor, who wants a real cricket bat. Sukhi is cooking. Bibi is listening to Partition-era radio songs. This hub returns twice more in the game, each time poorer.

**Turn.** A new SSP takes over Ghaziabad. Bablu, tipped off, moves the cash and the records. Daya is picked up on the Loni flyover under the Gangsters Act with ₹11 lakh in the car. Bablu does not take his call.

**Fade out:** Daya in Dasna jail, counting the bars.

## Act 2 — The Fall and the Begging Bowl (2019–2022)

Act 2 is the game's heart and its slowest section: two to three hours in which the player learns to survive on ₹60 a day.

**The fall, in three short scenes.**
- **Dasna jail, 2020.** Daya's property is attached. Bablu's lawyer offers to pay for silence; Daya refuses out of pride. He is beaten in the barracks for being a "Fortuner-wala" with no money left.
- **Release, 2021.** Nobody from the old life meets him at the gate. Sukhi has moved the family to a one-room rental in Khoda Colony. The tiffin service makes ₹9,000 a month. Gurnoor still has the plastic bat.
- **The job hunt.** Every door Daya knocks on knows his name. A transport owner laughs at him. A security agency wants a police verification he can never pass. He takes a loan from a local lender at 10 percent a month to buy an e-rickshaw; it is seized in six weeks.

**The platform.** Ashamed to go home empty-handed, Daya starts begging at Nizamuddin railway station. This is the core Act 2 gameplay loop: a day runs on the game clock, hunger and heat are real meters, and the player learns the platform from Mangal, a legless beggar who has worked it for 20 years.

What the player learns, because the game forces it:
- Begging is a business with a hierarchy. Platform 1 belongs to a group that pays a constable. Newcomers get the subway and the overbridge.
- A good day is ₹300. A train strike is ₹40. A festival is ₹900 and three fights.
- Food costs ₹50. A night at the shelter costs ₹10 or a favour. Medicine for a fever costs a day's earnings. The maths never works, which is the point.
- Police drives empty the station every two weeks; a baton to the knee halves Daya's walking speed for three in-game days.
- Being invisible has a use. Daya watches a pickpocket ring, a drug courier and a man who sells SIM cards without IDs. He notices because nobody thinks to hide from a beggar.

**Turn.** A quiet man in a kurta buys chai at the stall opposite Daya's spot three days running. On the fourth day he sits down next to him, speaks in Lahori Punjabi, and asks where his grandmother was born. The man is Devraj Khanna. Daya does not know this yet.

**Fade out:** Daya walking home with ₹700 he did not earn, unable to explain it to Sukhi.

## Act 3 — Recruitment and Training (2022)

Act 3 is short, 60 to 90 minutes, and exists to make the player understand what they are being asked to become.

**The pitch.** Ravi Saxena, not Khanna, makes the formal approach in a Sarai Kale Khan dhaba. He lays out the file: a terror module is being funded through a Karachi hawala network, the Sattar group. The agency has signals intelligence and satellite photos and no human inside. They need a man who can speak Kasur Punjabi, understand hawala, and survive a gang for a year without a weapon. The pay is ₹40,000 a month to the family, paid as a "pension" from a fake trust, and a clean record on return. Daya asks for ₹1 lakh. Ravi says ₹60,000 and the school fees. Daya cracks his left thumb and says yes.

**The farm.** A rented farmhouse outside Dehradun, four months. No gym montage. Training is:
- **Legend building.** Daya becomes Dilawar Hussain, a Kasur-born Pakistani who spent 11 years as a labourer in Sharjah, was deported after a fight, and has no family left. He learns Dilawar's childhood street by street from an old defector's memory.
- **Tradecraft** at the level a beggar can use: dead drops in a city with no post, burning a phone, a four-word distress code sent by buying a specific brand of cigarettes at a specific shop.
- **The Urdu press, Pakistani cricket, prayer.** He learns to pray correctly, which Bibi, a Sikh, finds unbearable when he practises at home.
- **One rule**, repeated until he hates it: never carry a weapon in the first six months. A man with a gun is a man with something to hide.

**Khanna's scene.** On the last night Khanna visits. He tells Daya about a friend who went across in 1985 and came back in 1992 to find his mother had died believing he was a traitor. He says the state will not remember Daya, but Khanna will. Daya asks if the friend was him. Khanna leaves without answering.

**The goodbye.** Daya tells Sukhi he has a two-year contract driving trucks in Oman. Gurnoor gets a real cricket bat. Bibi, who has guessed, gives him her Kasur address written in Urdu and says: if you see the house, do not go in.

**Route in** (realistic, shown as a travel sequence, not played): Delhi to Kathmandu by bus, Kathmandu to Sharjah on an Indian passport under another name, three weeks as a labourer in Sharjah to seed the legend, then Sharjah to Karachi on Dilawar's genuine-looking CNIC and a deportation order the agency has bought from a UAE clerk. He lands at Jinnah International with 4,000 Pakistani rupees and a plastic bag.

## Act 4 — Karachi (2023–2024)

Act 4 is half the game, six to eight hours, in a single open district: Gulbahar Lines, a fictional working-class neighbourhood modelled on the density of Lyari and Orangi. The player builds a life, a cover and a gang career from nothing, under constant suspicion.

**Phase 1 — Nobody (months 1 to 3).** Daya arrives with no contacts and sleeps outside a shrine. He begs, then carries water for a tanker operator, then runs tea for a gambling den. The Act 2 skills are the Act 4 skills: he is invisible, and he watches. He identifies the Sattar network by following the tankers, not by any intelligence the agency gave him. First dead drop: a chalk mark on a specific wall tells Ravi he is alive.

**Phase 2 — Runner (months 4 to 9).** Shahzaib, Sattar's nephew, notices the quiet Punjabi who never asks for a cut and never drinks. Daya becomes a runner: carrying cash between hawala shops, counting it, noticing the tokens and the code words. The hawala minigame from Act 1 returns from the other side. The player must reconstruct the money chain in a notebook the game lets you keep; a wrong entry can get you killed six months later.

**Phase 3 — Inside (months 10 to 16).** Daya is made a lieutenant after he takes a beating for Shahzaib during a turf fight and does not name names to the Rangers. He now meets Haji Sattar himself and sees the ledger: a line of money that leaves Karachi for the Gulf, comes back as charity, and funds a madrasa that is not a madrasa. Ravi, now in Karachi under a trader's cover, meets him once, at a cricket ground, for eleven minutes.

**The antagonist's arc.** Major Faisal Qureshi of ISI counter-intelligence appears in Phase 2 as a polite man who drinks tea with Sattar. He is certain India has a man inside and has narrowed it to four people. Across Phase 3 the player is tested without knowing it:
- A fake Indian agent approaches Daya at a bus stop. Taking the bait ends the game.
- Qureshi asks Daya, casually, what the Kasur railway station was called before 1990. The game gives the player eight seconds and the right answer is in Bibi's letter.
- Qureshi notices Daya crack his thumb when asked about Sharjah.

**The betrayal.** Shahzaib, impatient with his uncle's caution, plans to take the network and cut a direct deal with the ISI. Daya, who by now loves Shahzaib like a younger brother, has to choose: warn Sattar, or let Shahzaib kill him and use the chaos to photograph the ledger. The game lets the player choose; both paths reach the same ledger, but the Shahzaib path leaves Daya complicit in a murder and changes the tone of every later Shahzaib scene.

**The raid.** Daya passes the ledger to Ravi through a dead drop. The agency's move is not Indian: an anonymised tip goes to the Sindh Rangers, who raid Gulbahar Lines for their own reasons. The raid is the act's set piece and is played as a survival sequence, not a shootout: Daya has to get out of a neighbourhood under cordon, at night, without a weapon, with Shahzaib being dragged into a Rangers vehicle 40 metres away.

**Turn.** Qureshi, reviewing the four names after the raid, crosses out three. Daya has 72 hours.

## Act 5 — Extraction and the Return (2024)

Act 5 is 90 minutes of running, and the point where the cough the player has been ignoring since Phase 1 stops being cosmetic.

**The disease, seeded earlier.** Daya slept four months outside a shrine and six months in a gambling den with 20 men in a room. In Phase 2 he develops a cough. In Phase 3 he loses weight he cannot afford and sweats at night. The game shows this only in the stamina meter, which drains faster each month, and in Shahzaib telling him to see a doctor. He never does, because a Pakistani clinic means a CNIC check. It is pulmonary tuberculosis, untreated for over a year. The player is not told this until Act 6.

**The run.** With Qureshi's net closing, Ravi pulls Daya out through the only route that does not pass an airport: Karachi to the coast at Ibrahim Hyderi, a fishing boat across the creek, and an Indian Coast Guard pickup off Gujarat. The sequence is played at night with a 20-minute real-time window set by the tide.
- Rangers checkpoint on the Korangi road. Talk, bribe or walk. A gun ends it.
- The boat captain wants double. Daya has 11,000 rupees and a watch.
- Qureshi's men reach the jetty eight minutes after the boat leaves. Ravi, covering from the shore, is shot and does not make the boat. The player watches it from the water and cannot go back; the physics of a 40-horsepower boat against a tide do not allow it. Ravi's last radio message is the cigarette code for "go".

**The pickup.** Daya is lifted onto a Coast Guard vessel, coughing blood into the sea. He is 29 kg lighter than in Act 1. A doctor on board takes one look at him and says one word in Hindi that the player does not hear over the engine.

**The return.** Daya is debriefed for nine days in a Delhi safe house. The ledger photographs are good. Three financiers are picked up in Dubai; the module in India is rolled up before it moves. He is told the operation does not exist, that he was never in Pakistan, and that the pension will continue. Khanna comes on the ninth night, sits with him, and says nothing for a long time. Then he tells Daya he is free to go home.

**Fade out:** the gate of the Khoda Colony rental, a police tape across it, two weeks old.

## Act 6 — The Empty House and the Balcony (2024–2025)

Act 6 is the game's ending, three hours: the empty house, the hunt, and a fourth-floor balcony in Ghaziabad.

**The empty house.** Sukhi, Gurnoor and Bibi were killed 16 days before Daya came home, in the one-room rental, by two men who told the neighbours they were from the pension trust. The police file says robbery. There was nothing to rob. Daya walks the room in the same hub the player has visited three times. The cricket bat is still there. This is the longest non-interactive sequence in the game, four minutes with no music.

**What the agency knew.** Daya learns from a junior officer, drunk and ashamed, that the agency knew within three days. Khanna chose not to tell him because he was 72 hours from the ledger and the extraction window would have closed. The game gives the player one choice here: confront Khanna, or never see him again. Confronting him produces the game's hardest scene: Khanna does not apologise. He says he would do it again, that a hundred families are alive because Daya finished, and that he will carry Daya's three. Daya leaves without speaking.

**The hunt.** With no agency, no money beyond the pension and a body that is failing, Daya spends eight months finding the killers. This is the Act 2 loop turned into an investigation: patience, invisibility, small amounts of cash. The player works it piece by piece.
1. The neighbours' description. One of the men had a Ghaziabad transport-union tattoo.
2. The union's muster roll, bought from a clerk for ₹2,000.
3. The two men themselves, found in a Loni dhaba. One is killed by Daya, badly and up close, in a scene the game refuses to make clean. The other, dying, names who paid: Bablu Tyagi, on a call from a Karachi number.
4. The Karachi number. Daya works out, from the ledger he memorised, that it belongs to a hawala shop Qureshi used. Qureshi identified Daya from the thumb tell, traced the pension trust to Khoda Colony, and bought the hit through Bablu, who had wanted Daya's family gone since 2019.

**The diagnosis.** Midway through the hunt Daya collapses at a bus stop. A government hospital doctor tells him it is advanced tuberculosis with pleural effusion, that he has perhaps a year with treatment, and that he has stopped breathing properly because one lung is half full of fluid. Daya takes the medicine for three weeks, then stops, because the side effects slow him down. The stamina meter now has a hard ceiling that drops every in-game week.

**The balcony.** Bablu lives in a fourth-floor builder flat in Vasundhara, two streets from Daya's old house. Daya goes in at 2 a.m. through the stairwell, with the katta from Act 1 that he dug up from under a tree in Loni. The confrontation is played, not cut:
- Bablu wakes. Bablu is 20 kg heavier and healthy. Daya is faster for the first ten seconds and then he is not.
- The fight moves from bedroom to balcony. The player is winning, the katta at Bablu's throat, when a coughing fit hits: the screen tunnels, the controls go heavy, Daya's grip opens.
- Bablu shoves once. The balcony rail is a cheap builder-flat rail, 90 cm high. Daya's centre of mass is above it. He goes over.
- Four floors is roughly 12 metres and 1.5 seconds. The camera does not cut.

**The rewind.** As Daya goes over the rail the game rewinds for the first and only time: the fall from Daya's eyes, slowed to the game's only slow-motion. As he falls the player sees, in order: Gurnoor's bat, Bibi's Urdu letter, the chalk mark on the Karachi wall, Ravi on the jetty, the begging bowl at Nizamuddin, the Fortuner in 2019. The last thing he sees is the sky over Vasundhara. He lands. The game does not show the impact; it shows the pavement from street level, a dog, and a shutter rolling up as the milk delivery starts.

**Coda.** Three short cuts. Qureshi in Karachi reads a one-line Indian news report about an unidentified man found dead in Ghaziabad, and writes Daya's real name in the margin. Khanna, at his desk, is told, and asks that the pension be paid to the gurdwara in Mamdot in perpetuity. Mangal at Nizamuddin, on his platform, counts ₹340 and finds a ₹500 note he does not remember receiving.

**Player choice that survives the ending.** Whether Daya warned Sattar or let Shahzaib kill him in Act 4 decides one line in the coda: in the Shahzaib path, Shahzaib is shown alive in Rangers custody, reading the same news report. In the Sattar path, that cut is of his grave.

## Physics and realism rules

Every rule below exists because the story needs it; none is realism for its own sake.

| System | Rule | Why the story needs it |
|---|---|---|
| Health | No regeneration. Injuries heal over in-game days, with a doctor, or not at all. A knee hit in Act 2 is still slower in Act 4. | The body is a ledger. The balcony fall is the last entry. |
| Stamina and the disease | A single stamina meter drives running, fighting and climbing. From Act 4 Phase 2 its maximum shrinks every in-game month; from the Act 6 diagnosis it drops weekly. Coughing fits trigger at low stamina and lock input for 2 to 4 seconds. | The ending is caused by a mechanic the player has felt for ten hours, not by a cutscene. |
| Money | Every rupee is tracked. No abstract score. Prices are real: chai ₹10, a katta ₹8,000, a clerk ₹2,000, a boat 11,000 PKR. Running out of money is the most common fail state in Acts 2 and 6. | The hero's whole life is an argument about what money can buy. |
| Guns | Bullet drop, penetration by material (brick stops a 9 mm, a car door does not), no aim assist, 20 to 30 rounds in the whole Karachi act. A katta misfires 1 in 8 times. Firing a gun in a populated area summons police or Rangers within 3 to 5 minutes and marks the district hot for a week. | Daya is not a shooter. The one gun in Act 6 fails him. |
| Hand to hand | Weight and reach matter. A heavier opponent wins a clinch unless the player breaks it in the first seconds. Fights last 10 to 20 seconds and leave lasting injuries. | Bablu beats Daya because Bablu is heavier and healthy. |
| Falls | Rigid-body ragdoll with real mass. Above 3 metres, injury; above 6, likely death. Rails have real heights (90 cm builder rail versus 110 cm code rail) and a character's centre of mass can go over. | The balcony has to be plausibly lethal and plausibly the rail's fault. |
| Vehicles | Mass, braking distance and traffic. A Fortuner in Act 1 stops in 40 m from 80 km/h; a motorbike in Karachi traffic averages 18 km/h. No ramps, no jumps. | Chases are about traffic and nerve, not stunts. |
| Boat | The Act 5 boat does 25 km/h against a 4 km/h ebb; turning back costs 6 minutes the tide does not allow. | Ravi's death must be the physics, not the player's cowardice. |
| Heat and hunger | Delhi in June and Karachi in August drain stamina in the open. Hunger past 18 hours halves stamina regeneration. | Act 2 has to hurt. |
| Suspicion | A hidden per-NPC suspicion value in Karachi rises from accent slips, hesitation on legend questions, carrying a weapon early, and the thumb tell. It never shows on the HUD. | Being found out should feel like it came from nowhere, as it would. |
| Time | A real calendar runs across the game. The disease, the pension, the tide, the Rangers drives all follow it. | The player should feel three years pass. |
| Information | No minimap in Karachi. The player draws their own map and hawala chain in an in-game notebook; mistakes persist. | Spying is remembering correctly. |
| Saves | Checkpoints only at the end of each in-game day. No reload to undo a bad conversation. | Consequences are the design. |

## Themes, sensitivity and production notes

**Themes.** Visibility and invisibility. The price of being useful. The state as a patient, grateful, unforgiving employer. Money as the only language Daya ever trusted and the one that failed him three times.

**Sensitivity.** The spymaster is fictional; no real official, serving or retired, is depicted or named. Karachi is shown as a real city with real people, not a villain's lair; the Sattar gang are criminals, the neighbours are not. Qureshi is competent and courteous, never a caricature. Begging is shown as labour, not comedy. Tuberculosis is written accurately: a disease of crowding and poverty, treatable if caught, lethal if ignored.

**Language.** Hinglish and Punjabi in Delhi; Urdu, Punjabi and Sindhi-inflected Urdu in Karachi. Subtitles always on. Daya's accent audibly shifts between acts.

**Music.** Almost none. Diegetic radio, a shrine qawwali, the Partition songs Bibi plays. One original theme, heard in full only during the rewind.
